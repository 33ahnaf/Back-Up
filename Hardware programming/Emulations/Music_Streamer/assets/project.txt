Describe the environmental issue in your community that you are trying to solve. (150 words maximum)

The Teesta River basin in Northern Bangladesh, specifically the Rangpur region, suffers from erratic flooding and severe riverbank erosion. A critical but often overlooked factor is siltation—the accumulation of sediment that raises the riverbed, reducing its water-carrying capacity and making flash floods more frequent and unpredictable. Current warning systems are either non-existent or rely on manual observations that are too slow for sudden riparian disasters. This leads to massive crop destruction, loss of livestock, and displacement of marginalized riverside communities. There is an urgent need for a high-precision, real-time monitoring system that can predict overflows by analyzing the synergy between water levels, flow velocity, and sediment density.

Apart from your team, who else will be involved in implementing the project?

To ensure the project's success, we will collaborate with:

Bangladesh Water Development Board (BWDB): For access to historical hydrological data and technical guidance on river behavior.

Local Farmers and Community Leaders: To identify high-risk zones and test the effectiveness of the alert system.

How does your project address the problem? Explain your proposed solution. (150 words maximum)

Project TEESTA-SENTRY implements a "Digital Twin" of the river using a three-layer architecture. First, solar-powered IoT Edge-Nodes (equipped with ultrasonic, turbidity, and velocity sensors) are deployed upstream to collect real-time hydrological data. This data is transmitted via LoRaWAN—a long-range, low-power network—to a central Raspberry Pi server. Second, an LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) Neural Network analyzes this time-series data to predict flood risks based on siltation levels and rainfall patterns. Finally, the system triggers an Omni-channel Alert Protocol, sending automated SMS alerts to farmers and activating village sirens. By moving from reactive monitoring to predictive analysis, TEESTA-SENTRY provides the critical lead time needed to save lives and livelihoods.

How does your project use E-STEM (Environmental Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) or indigenous/local knowledge to address the problem you mentioned?

Environmental Science: We apply principles of Hydrology to understand how siltation (sediment transport) affects river discharge and flood probability.

Technology: We utilize IoT (Internet of Things) for data acquisition, LoRaWAN for resilient communication in disaster zones, and Deep Learning (LSTM) for predictive modeling.

Engineering: The project involves designing waterproof, solar-powered enclosures for sensors and building a centralized server architecture using a Raspberry Pi.

Mathematics: We use Multivariate Analysis and Time-Series Forecasting to calculate the correlation between turbidity, water velocity, and the likelihood of a breach.

Local Knowledge: We integrate community input on historical "flood marks" and traditional crop cycles to calibrate our alert thresholds.

What positive change do you expect from this project? (100 words maximum)

We expect a significant reduction in economic loss for Rangpur's farmers by providing a 24–72 hour early warning window, allowing them to harvest crops early or move livestock. Environmentally, the project provides a "Siltation Map," enabling the government to perform targeted dredging rather than wasteful, broad dredging. Most importantly, it will reduce the loss of human life by replacing guesswork with data-driven evacuation triggers, fostering a more climate-resilient community in the face of an erratic Teesta river.

How will you know if your project is successful? (100 words maximum)

Success will be measured by:

Technical Validation: The prototype successfully transmitting data from the river to the server via LoRaWAN.

Predictive Accuracy: The LSTM model achieving a high correlation (low error rate) between predicted and actual water level spikes.

Reach: The number of farmers successfully receiving and acknowledging the SMS alerts during tests.

Response Time: Reducing the time between a "danger signature" detection and the community alert to under 5 minutes.

Is this idea completely new, or has something similar been tried before? (If yes, briefly explain.)

While basic water-level sensors exist, TEESTA-SENTRY is revolutionary because it integrates Siltation (Turbidity) analysis with Deep Learning (LSTM). Most existing systems are "threshold-based" (they alarm when the water is high). Our system is "predictive" (it alarms because the conditions suggest the water will be high). Furthermore, the use of LoRaWAN ensures the system remains operational even when cellular networks fail during storms, a critical flaw in current regional systems.
